<p align="justify">People often talk about how the internet has changed this society to a good way. They can do homework faster, work faster, everything can be done in a minute with ease and efficiency. It's also brought a vast job opportunities. Back then, people had to search for job applications through newspapers, magazines, or even posters on power poles which sometimes took effort to apply such jobs. Once you found a job application, you had to go to the place in person, filled out forms by hand, and waited for days, weeks, or even months to hear the news back. Today, a person can search through hundreds of job applications, submit their application, and communicate with employers without ever leaving their home. In many ways, the internet has made life more convenient, connected, and productive than ever.</p>

<p align="justify">However, the internet isn't her without its downs and pitfalls. One of the biggest changes it's brought is something most people don't notice, it's the fact that we've lost our ability to be bored. In the past, boredom was a normal part of people's life. When people had nothing to do, they sat alone with their thoughts, wondering about the meaning of life. They'd stare out the window for hours, go for a walk, daydream, talk to themselves, reflect on their mistakes, and think about the future. Although boredom was often seen as unpleasant, it provided people time and space to think deeply.</p>

<p align="justify">Today, however, things are so much different. Boredom has become a plague that must be cured and found the right medication for immediately, instead of being used as a powerful tool. The moment boredom appears, many people immediately reach for their phones. Social media, short-form contents, and online games have made it possible to avoid boredom almost entirely. Every gap or void they find within their activities can be filled with entertainment, leaving a little to no room for boredom to sit within. Even when they're already doing a particular activity, if they can add another layer of entertainment, they'll do it right away. Taking a poo, waiting for a bus, standing in queue, eating alone, driving a car, moments that once allowed people to sit with their thoughts are now occupied by endless scrolling through screen.</p>

<p align="justify">One thing that genuinely surprised me was discovering how many people use their phones while sitting on the toilet. For them, even those few minutes of silence and boredom have to be filled with entertainment right away. In contrast, I usually spend that time alone with my thoughts, sometimes thinking about random ideas, reflecting my days, hallucinating about how my future will be going, or even talking to myself like I'm nuts. The same thing happens while driving. Many people listen to music, podcasts, or other forms of entertainment to keep themselves entertained. Meanwhile, I often drive in complete silence, accompanied by my own thoughts. To some people, those moments might be boring, but I always find peace within those. Those are some of the only times when I can have peace with my own thoughts in a fast-paced society filled with noise and simultaneity.</p>

<p align="justify">By writing this, I'm not saying that entertainment is always bad, sometimes we need it to refresh our minds after working hard, even I do this sometimes. However, I believe that we've become too afraid of boredom. We treat every moment of silence and boredom as something hollow that needs to be filled, when in reality, those moments can be some of the most beneficial and crucial parts of our day. Including this, I wouldn't write this without embracing little moments of silence and boredom within my activities. The idea of writing this came from the most boring moments like taking a poo, sitting by myself, driving back home, or even daydreaming. Boredom gives us the opportunity that no moment does to reflect, question, and understand our lives and ourselves better.</p>
