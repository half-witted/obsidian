<p align="justify">People often talk about how the internet has changed the society in a good way. They can do homework faster, work faster, everything can be done in a minute with ease and efficiency. It’s also brought vast job opportunities. Back then, people had to search for job applications through newspapers, magazines, or even posters on power poles which sometimes took effort to apply such jobs. Once you found a job application, you had to go to the place in person, fill out forms by hand, and wait days, weeks, or even months to hear the news back. Today, a person can search through hundreds of job applications (like Linkedin, you name it), submit applications, and communicate with employers without ever leaving their home. In many ways, the internet has made life more convenient, connected, and productive than ever.</p>
<p align="justify">However, the internet isn’t here without its downs and pitfalls. One of the biggest changes it has brought is something most people don’t notice, it’s the fact that we’ve lost our ability to be bored. In the past, boredom was a normal part of people’s life. When people had nothing to do, they sat alone with their thoughts, wondering about the meaning of life. They stared out the window, went for walks, daydreamed, talked to themselves, reflected on their mistakes, and thought about their future. Although boredom was often seen as unpleasant, it provided people with the time and space to think deeply.</p>
<p align="justify">Many of history’s greatest philosophers spent long periods of their lives in contemplation and solitude. Boredom wasn’t something they constantly tried to escape. Instead, it had become a crucial and inevitable part of their lives, being a daily routine. Those quiet moments allowed them to question the world around them, develop new ideas, and generate thoughts that would influence future generations. Rather than viewing boredom as a problem, they made it a powerful tool for reflection, creativity, and intellectual growth.</p>
<p align="justify">Today, however, things are so much different. Boredom has become a plague that must be cured and found the right medication for immediately, instead of being used as a powerful tool. The moment boredom appears, many people immediately reach for their phones. Social media, short-form contents, and online games have made it possible to avoid boredom almost entirely. Every gap or void they find within their activities can be filled with entertainment, leaving a little to no room for boredom to sit within. Even when they’re already doing a particular activity, if they can add another layer of entertainment, they’ll do it right away. Taking a poo, waiting for a bus, standing in line, eating alone, driving a car or a motorcycle, moments that once allowed people to sit with their thoughts are now occupied by endless scrolling through a screen.</p>
<p align="justify">One thing that genuinely surprised me was discovering how many people use their phones while sitting on the toilet. For them, even those few minutes of silence and boredom have to be filled with entertainment right away. Meanwhile, I usually spend that time alone with my thoughts, sometimes thinking about random ideas, reflecting my days, making hallucinations about how my future will be going, or even talking to myself like I’m nuts. The same thing happens while driving. Many people listen to music, podcasts, or other forms of entertainment to keep themselves entertained. Meanwhile, I often drive in complete silence, accompanied by my own thoughts. To some people, those moments might be boring, but I have always found peace within those. Those are some of the only times when I can have peace with my own thoughts in a fast-paced society filled with noise.</p>
<p align="justify">By writing this, I’m not saying that entertainment is always bad, sometimes we need it to refresh our minds. However, I believe that we’ve become too afraid of boredom. We treat every moment of silence and boredom as something hollow that needs to be filled, when in reality those moments can be some of the most beneficial and crucial parts of our day. Including this, I wouldn’t have written this without embracing little moments of silence and boredom within my activities. I got the idea and thoughts for writing this from the most boring moments like taking a poo, sitting by myself, driving back home, or even daydreaming. Boredom gives us the opportunity that no moment does to reflect, question, and understand ourselves better.</p>
