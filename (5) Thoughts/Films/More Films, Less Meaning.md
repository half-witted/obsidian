<p align="justify">We live in a time when there are more films available to us than ever before, yet we may be paying less attention to them than before. People now play films on a secondary screen while they scroll through their phones or do any other activities that can be accompanied by another layer of entertainment. It shouldn't be treated like that, it does way more than that. A film can show us the life we've never lived and sometimes, we can take lessons better than we do in classes. We can understand someone we never think we'd understand and parts of life we never think we'd need. Yet, despite all of this, we often fail to do so. Why? </p>
<p align="justify">In early stages of cinema, films were delivered only through big screens. People had to really commit to go to such places and really experience to the fullest of the films. People often went to the cinema without ever thinking about which films they should watch, they just went to the cinema and chose the film they wanted to watch in place. After they finished watching, they could go to casinos, pools, or bars near the cinema to mingle with one another. Therefore, going to the cinema wasn't just about watching a film, but also about spending time and making the most of it with other people. Cinemas became cultural places, they became a part of people's social lives where people gathered, mingled, socialised, and shared an experience together, or just simply met new people.</p>
<p align="justify">Films can be the mediums for people to learn more about life, the kind of lesson they can't get in regular classes. Yes, people can learn about society in classes, but films can be far more efficient at delivering such messages by displaying people's experiences. A lesson about grief, love, loneliness, or any of those lessons can be understood through definitions and explanations, but seeing a character experience them can make those lessons more understandable. There is a quote that goes "Experience is the teacher of all things." by Julius Caesar. Therefore, although people don't experience those feelings, films can allow them to experience them through someone else's view.</p>
<p align="justify">It all began to change as streaming platforms setting a new standard around 2007. From being an activity that people have to really set aside time to do so, watching films can now be done at home with ease. People just have to open their streaming platforms on their phones and can immediately start watching the films of their choice. People with a busy schedule can finally watch films without ever disturbing their routines. It provides beneficial convenience, but it also changed the way people interacted with films. This also results in changes of how cinemas play a role in society. What was once a place where people had time and socialised with each other has become less central to people's social lives.  As there are many ways to watch films, people no longer have the reason to go to a cinema. Instead of thinking, "Let's go the cinema," people can simply think, "I can watch films at home, why should I?" Cinemas still exist, but they've lost their true meaning in society and become an optional activity.</p>
<p align="justify">Not only streaming platforms, social media also contributes in affecting how people view films. As the access become as easy as ever, they begin to treat them as something petty. They watch films to cure boredom, to accompany boring activities like eating, pooping, etc, or simply to have something playing in the background while they do something else. Instead of making watching films as an activity they give full attention to, they consider it as something to fill the gap between activities. It has become an activity to do when people have time, rather time an activity when people set aside them to do so.</p><p align="justify">You may notice this pattern as you scroll through social media. Many content creators put films alongside boring lessons so that people can learn the lesson without being bored. I find this very often where there are videos of podcast that have films, subway surfers video, sand or soap videos alongside them. Although films have always been a form of entertainment, their ability to teach people about important lessons about life can easily be overlooked when entertainment becomes their primary purpose. Films aren't only something to watch for entertainment, they can also be a way for people to learn more about life. When the entertainment aspect becomes more dominant, the other side of films can be forgotten easily.</p>
<p align="justify">This condition is worsened by the way big companies prioritise commercial success. They begin to think about how their films can attract as many viewers as possible and get as much profits as possible rather than how they can provide meaningful lessons. As a result, you may find many big-budget films that focus heavily on entertainment and mass appeal while having little meaningful lessons behind them. The goal isn't to make people understand something new anymore, but to make them stay entertained and keep watching. Films franchises such as Fast & Furious, Mission: Impossible, etc are examples of how big companies try to tie their audiences as long as possible by milking the same idea over and over again. While sequels aren't necessarily bad, repeatedly using on the same idea can make commercial success more important than exploring new ideas.</p>
<p align="justify">Films have always been a form of entertainment, but they've also been much more than that. They can allow us to understand lessons better by watching someone else's experience than in regular classes in some aspects. However, the way we consume films has changed. Streaming platforms have made films more accessible, social media has encouraged passive watching, and commercial interests have made entertainment, mass appeal, and profits more important than the messages the films deliver. The problem isn't the films themselves, but how we have altered the role films play in society. We may have more films to watch, more ways to watch them than ever. But if we continue this pattern we also gain less from them than ever before.</p>
