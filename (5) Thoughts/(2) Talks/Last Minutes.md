<p align="justify">When you die, your brain would spend exactly seven minutes replaying your life. Seven minutes to relive everything mattered before you never get to again. And I've got no idea why the brain would do that, but I reckon I know how mine would begin.</p>
<p align="justify">The first minute would take me back to my birth. I'd watch my mother bring me into the world of the living, hold me when I was helpless, care for me when I was nothing but a living liability. I'd see my father work hard endlessly just to make my birth possible, to put a spoonful of rice though blood and sweat were the very price to pay. I'd feel thankful to my sister for choosing my name, a gift I carry every day, the thing that would be written on my tombstone as a proof I once existed. I'd look at my family and wonder how they felt in that very moment. Were they happy? Hopeful? This first minute would show me where everything started, when the world quietly changed because I arrived in it.</p>
<p align="justify">The second minute would belong to my childhood. The time when happiness came so easily. When sleeping felt like a punishment and playing games all day felt completely fine, before reality came through. I'd remember how my birthdays were still celebrated, how special they felt back then, the kind of joy only the child version of me could feel. That minute would pass too fast, way too fast and I'd wish it would stay longer.</p>
<p align="justify">I'd walk into the minute number three that would bring me to my teenage years. The moment reality began to interrupt my innocence. I'd remember the friends I made, the late-night drives, and the careless laughter. I'd also remember the friends I lost, not only to bad communication and distance, but to illnesses we couldn't cure and signs we didn't fully comprehend soon enough. I'd also remember the first time I knew and met my first and only true love, when I learned that love wasn't only about dating and desire, but about choosing to grow, to be better, for someone else. That minute would hold both the most pain and the most joy, and I'd realise that the pain was shaping a future I'm grateful for.</p>
<p align="justify">I don't know what minute four, five, six, or seven would show me for my story isn't done yet. But I know one thing for sure: there's no joy without pain. This really align about 'Komorebi' in a movie titled [[Perfect Days]] where joy and pain run through the same current. Life isn't just about the good, but also the long, difficult process where I lost people and things without warning, and the ones I found when I never thought I would. And maybe, that's what makes my life worth remembering.</p>
